# Transfer graph: first look at September 2026

2026-10-10. Epic E2 of the PRD "XRPL Transfer Graph Analysis". Account-level graph (before entity
grouping) built from gold `transfer_edges_monthly`. Full tables: `graph-first-look-2026-09-tables.md`
(generated by `uv run graph/run.py firstlook --month 2026-09`). Prototype picture: `report/graph_proto.html`.

## Findings

1. **The XRP graph is one connected network.** 381,087 accounts and 1,682,754 directed edges carry
   10,659,498 XRP transfers; 99.9% of accounts are in one weakly connected component. RLUSD is much smaller
   and lumpier: 4,903 accounts and 8,291 edges carry 6,915,024 transfers (83% in the largest component), so
   a few pairs repeat transfers at very high rates.
2. **Exchanges show up as pairs of accounts.** Most of the top hubs are a *deposit* wallet (≥ 99% of incoming
   transfers carry a destination tag, thousands of distinct tags, almost no outgoing edges) next to a
   *withdrawal* wallet (few senders, thousands of receivers). Example: `rNxp4h8a…` (29,348 tags, out-degree 1)
   and `rDAE53Vf…` (out-degree 3,973) each moved about 1.32 billion XRP in the month. Among the top 50 XRP
   hubs by PageRank: 24 likely custodial, 2 bot pairs, 1 issuer, 23 unknown, and most of the unknown ones look
   like withdrawal wallets. The entity rules (E3) do **not** join deposit and withdrawal wallets; doing that
   would need a new, reviewed rule (deposit → withdrawal sweeps).
3. **Withdrawal wallets are also the biggest funders.** `rnJrjec2…`, a top-10 XRP hub with 14,827 receivers,
   created 47,321 new accounts in the window. This is why "same funder" can never mean "same owner".
4. **Degree tails are heavy, but not cleanly power laws.** In-degree α = 2.77 (x_min 16) and out-degree
   α = 1.77 (x_min 8) for XRP: Pareto indices 1.77 and 0.77 against the ≈ 1 reported for 2013–2019. The
   likelihood ratio against a lognormal is not significant (R = −0.89, p = 0.38), so the tail is equally
   well described by a lognormal. RLUSD comes out closest to the old baseline (Pareto indices 1.09 / 1.18).
5. **Value-weighted PageRank rewards sinks.** The top RLUSD node by PageRank (`rGGXaBdS…`) received 2,023
   tiny transfers (350 RLUSD in total) and sent nothing. PageRank should be read next to degree and value,
   never alone; the views size nodes by value moved, not by PageRank.
6. **Bots are visible directly.** One RLUSD pair (`rPTpLbCY…` → `rGZxn437…`) repeats 245 k transfers a month
   with 94 k going back; in the entity graph, 2.2 M of the month's 18.9 M transfers turn out to be inside
   grouped entities (funding trees that only pay each other).

## Cost against the Architecture budget

| Step (Sept 2026) | Measured | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| Edge aggregation (gold SQL) | 24.5 s alone; 30–140 s per month in the backfill | < 1 min (over on the busiest months, under the 10 min per call limit) |
| Load XRP account graph into igraph | 4.0 s, 1.25 GB RSS | < 1 GB (slightly over: igraph built from Python lists) |
| PageRank, components, degrees (XRP) | 3.6 s | < 1 min |
| Entities, one month | 17 s, 2.7 GB peak | — |
| All E4 metrics, 11 assets | 110 s, 3.95 GB peak | < 15 min |

Two estimates were badly wrong and were fixed before the full run: leidenalg took 108 s for 2 iterations
(igraph's built-in Leiden: 11 s and a better modularity), and the Hodge least squares took 153 s per asset
(Jacobi-preconditioned CG on the Laplacian: 0.9 s, same result).

## Decision

**Go for M2–M4.** The graph is cheap to compute on this host (minutes per month), the structure is
interpretable (exchange pairs, bot pairs, funders), and the loop share differs strongly between tokens
(1–3% for some, about 50–64% for others in September), which is the signal E4 and E5 are for.
Follow-ups: a reviewed deposit → withdrawal rule for exchanges; MPT issuance ids in silver `transfers`.
